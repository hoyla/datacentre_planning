 Cato Data Centre 
 
 Ahead in the cloud. 
 
 Built in Fife. Powered by Scotland. Setting the Standard for Sustainability. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Cato is the planned new green data centre, adjacent to the Mossmorran Gas Plant near Auchtertool in Fife. Powered by 100% renewable electricity. Designed for low water use. Shaped around genuine sustainability commitments. 
 
 This website sets out what Cato is, the high standard to which it has been designed, and outlines our wider commitments to sustainability and community benefits.

 What is a data centre? 
 
 When a photo saves from your phone to ‘the cloud’, or you stream on Netflix, search on Google, use Facebook, or use an AI assistant, all of that runs on a computer server, in a building, somewhere. A data centre then is a physical thing, a building, an actual place in the real world in which to store and process all the information that enriches our modern lives. But they are more than just buildings.They are high tech, environmentally controlled, precision engineered environments, staffed by highly skilled and highly qualified engineers working across a range of disciplines. Modern data centres also host and train AI systems, alongside high density computing workloads such as climate change modelling. Cato is one such development. A campus of data centres designed to be clean, green, and energy-efficient – pushing the industry to use the highest standards possible, as Scotland demands.

 Current Status 
 
 An application for Planning Permission in Principle (PPP) has been submitted to Fife Council under reference 26/01243/PPP . The current stage is public consultation on the PPP application. Comments can be submitted through the Fife Council Planning Portal until 24 th July. 
 
 
 
 
 
 What is Planning Permission in Principle? 
 
 Planning Permission in Principle is an initial approval that establishes whether a development of this type is acceptable in principle on this site. It fixes the important parameters – the use, the site boundary, the maximum scale, the access arrangements, and the broad landscape and environmental strategy. If PPP is granted, a second, more detailed application must be submitted and approved before construction can begin 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 Who decides? 
 
 The decision-maker is Fife Council. The application will be considered by the council’s planning committee, informed by: The recommendation of Fife Council’s Planning Service Responses from internal Fife Council consultees Responses from external statutory consultees Comments submitted by members of the public, community councils and other interested parties Because Cato is a project of national significance, Scottish Ministers reserve the right to call the application in for their own determination. If that happens, the process moves to a Reporter appointed by the Scottish Government. 
 
 
 
 How to take part 
 
 Comment on the application 
 
 The most direct route is to submit a formal comment through the Fife Council Planning Portal, quoting reference 26/01243/PPP . Comments can be for the application, against it, or neutral. All comments received during the consultation period are read and considered as part of the determination. The current consultation period runs until 24 th July.
