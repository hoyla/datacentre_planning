Two ways in, and they are not the same thing

Signals

Named queries over the adjudicated findings — cohorts of sites that share a measurable property. Each one shows its own definition and the script that produces it. No model chooses what appears, and no cohort is a conclusion.

The data

The full tables, unchanged: every site, application, energy project and operator, with every column, every caveat and every figure traceable to a document, a page and the model that read it.

Where this data can mislead

Each of these produced a wrong number during construction and was corrected. The same traps apply to anyone doing their own analysis over the workbook or the database.

What these are, and who wrote them

Each signal is a deterministic query over the adjudicated findings, defined in dcp/signal_families.py and re-run when the page is generated. The wording is a fixed template with the count substituted in; the ordering is cohort size weighted by reading coverage. No language model selected, ranked or described anything on this screen, and no cohort asserts a cause. A signal says only: these sites share this property, measured this way.

Interpretation belongs in a reporter’s note on the site itself, signed by the person who wrote it. Where a machine has drafted anything, it is boxed, labelled and collapsed.

Generation, cooling and water

{{ pl.label }} {{ pl.value }}

Generator counts are plant: the highest number disclosed in any one document, so phases described separately are not added together. Bracketed numbers count passages naming that fuel or method, not units installed — applications routinely compare options before choosing, and the count is what separates the method used from the methods considered. Cooling and fuel are not adjudicated for attribution, unlike the capacity figures.
