# Internal review request — Ofgem, FOI2026/01464

**To:** information.rights@ofgem.gov.uk, quoting FOI2026/01464.
**Deadline:** the request for review must reach Ofgem within 40 working
days of its 15 September 2026 letter — **by 10 November 2026**. Ofgem

**What it is for:** Ofgem applied two exceptions to four questions as a

the fallback the request offered. The review asks for the balance to be
struck field by field, and names three things Ofgem cannot lawfully

projects, and the letter says so.

Dear Information Rights team,

I am writing to request an internal review of Ofgem's response of 15 September 2026 to my request FOI2026/01464, under regulation 11 of the Environmental Information Regulations 2004. I am grateful for the response and for the confirmation that the request was handled under the Regulations. I ask the reviewer to reconsider points 1 to 6 on the following grounds.

**1. Regulation 12(5)(d) is not engaged by a dataset supplied by another authority.** The exception protects the confidentiality of *proceedings* where that confidentiality is provided by law. The Information Commissioner's guidance limits "proceedings" to formal deliberative processes, and requires the information itself to be part of those proceedings. What I requested is not a record of Ofgem's deliberation; it is the input data NESO collected from demand customers, which Ofgem then analysed. Its disclosure would reveal what the queue contains, not how Ofgem reasoned about it. Ofgem's own reasoning — that disclosure "would reveal information forming part of Ofgem's live assessment" — describes analysis the response does not identify, not the datasets at points 1 to 4. I ask the reviewer to identify which, if any, of the withheld information is a record of proceedings, and to release the remainder under regulation 12(11).

**2. The "chilling effect" on which both exceptions rest is factually wrong for the principal dataset.** The response says disclosure "would also discourage applicants from providing information to NESO as part of the connections applications process". The Information Request Notice of March 2026, which is the source of the Table 1 and Table 3 datasets and is named as such in Table 3's heading, was not voluntary. NESO's own page for it states that it "is being issued under NESO's statutory information gathering powers" and that "a complete and accurate response is required from all relevant demand customers". Information obtained under compulsion cannot be discouraged by disclosure, and the Commissioner has consistently given no weight to a chilling-effect argument in respect of information a person was legally obliged to provide.

**3. Regulation 12(5)(e) was applied to the whole of four datasets without the field-by-field assessment the exception requires.** Each of its four limbs must be met for the specific information withheld, and the harm must be shown to be more probable than not. The response asserts harm to "certain projects" in general terms and does not distinguish between:

- (a) a project's existence, name, connection site and contracted capacity, which for 119 transmission-connected demand projects NESO has already published, with each developer's consent, in its Existing Agreements register (https://www.neso.energy/document/373996/download, as at 11 June 2025: project name, capacity, connection date and connection point), and which, for any project with a planning application, is on a local authority's public register with the applicant's name and the stated grid capacity;
- (b) the size band and technology classification of a project, which Ofgem itself has published in aggregate in Tables 1 and 3; and
- (c) any genuinely commercial content, such as capital expenditure estimates or financing status, which was not requested at points 1, 3 or 4.

Information already in the public domain cannot be confidential, so limb two fails for (a); and for (b) the response's own conclusion that transparency "can be met through publication of aggregate data" concedes that aggregation removes the harm. Regulation 12(11) requires the information that is not exempt to be separated and disclosed. I ask the reviewer to disclose (a) and (b) for each project, and to state, for anything still withheld, which limb of the exception it fails and why.

**4. The fallback offered in the request was not considered.** My request asked, in the alternative, for "the same fields with developer names redacted but connection location (e.g. by local authority), capacity and classification retained". The response does not mention it. Regulation 9 requires advice and assistance, and regulation 12(11) requires partial disclosure where separation is reasonably practicable. A table of project rows carrying size band, local authority or transmission zone, current and previous technology classification, queue status and, for the paragraph 2.10 cohort, the month of reclassification, contains nothing that identifies a developer's commercial position. NESO has itself released exactly this form of information: its responses FOI/26/121 (11 August 2026) and FOI/25/294 (internal review, 1 June 2026) give the count, size distribution and month of receipt of battery-to-data-centre modifications, and FOI/26/113 (14 August 2026) gives transmission-connected demand by firm connection year. I ask that Ofgem provide points 1 to 4 in at least this form.

**5. Point 5 is held, on the response's own account.** The response says Ofgem obtains the data "by virtue of the provisions of the Utilities Act 2000, and Part 1 of the Electricity Act 1989". If the data was obtained under a statutory power, the exercise of that power is recorded — a notice, a direction, a licence-condition request or a standing arrangement — and that record is the "arrangement governing Ofgem's receipt and use" that point 5 asked for. I ask the reviewer to identify the provision and the record, and to disclose it, or to explain why no such record exists.

**6. Point 6 should be re-searched against the response's own statement.** The response reports that Ofgem plans to consult on an option to require network companies to publish a "Demand Capacity Register". A decision to consult on publishing queue data is not reached without correspondence with the body that holds the queue. I ask the reviewer to confirm what searches were made for point 6, and over what date range.

**7. The public interest balance did not weigh what was actually at stake.** The consultation calibrated a commitment fee and queue milestones on this evidence base and closed on 16 September 2026, with decisions expected later this year; consultees could not test the calibration. Communities are being consulted on individual planning applications for the same projects with no means of seeing what grid capacity the project has contracted for or under what classification it entered the queue. The £693 billion implied capital expenditure figure, around 23 per cent of UK GDP, rests on unpublished disaggregation. Against that, the response identifies no specific project and no specific harm. Regulation 12(2) requires the presumption in favour of disclosure to be applied; the response records it as a factor and then gives it no weight.

I would be grateful if the review could address each of the seven points above. If the review maintains any exception, I ask that the response state, for each field withheld, the exception relied on and the harm identified, so that any complaint to the Information Commissioner can be precise.

Yours faithfully,
