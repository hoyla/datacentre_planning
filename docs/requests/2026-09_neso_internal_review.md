Dear Information Rights team,

I am writing to request an internal review of NESO's response of 10 September 2026 to my request FOI/26/165, under regulation 11 of the Environmental Information Regulations 2004. I am grateful for a clear response, and in particular for the confirmation that NESO holds the four datasets at questions 1 to 4 and for the two earlier responses enclosed with it. I ask the reviewer to reconsider the following.

**1. The exception was applied to every field of every project without the separation regulation 12(11) requires.** Regulation 12(5)(e) applies "to the extent that" disclosure would adversely affect a confidentiality provided by law, and each of its four limbs must be met for the specific information withheld. The response engages it for "the project-level information that you have requested in questions 1 to 4" as a whole. It does not distinguish a project's name, connection site and contracted capacity from its financial commitment status, or a project whose existence is public from one whose is not. The response's own words — "where that information has not been made publicly available" — state the correct test and then do not apply it.

**2. For 119 of these projects NESO has already published the core fields.** The response states that NESO does "not currently publish project level information on transmission connected demand projects". NESO's Existing Agreements register (https://www.neso.energy/document/373996/download, as at 11 June 2025) lists, for every project that applied for Gate 2 and consented to inclusion, its project name, associated installed capacity, existing connection date and existing connection point, and 119 of those rows are transmission-connected demand. I accept that inclusion was by consent and that the register is static; that does not alter the point. Information a developer has agreed to make public, and NESO has published, is in the public domain, so the second limb of regulation 12(5)(e) cannot be met for the same fields, for the same projects, in the Information Request Notice data. Nor can it be met for the existence, name and capacity of a data-centre project that has applied for planning permission, which a local authority publishes with the applicant's name. I ask the reviewer to disclose, for each project in the IRN data whose name and capacity are already public in either of those ways, those fields and the IRN's data-centre yes/no answer beside them: a classification cannot be commercially confidential once the project it attaches to is public.

**3. Section 105 of the Utilities Act 2000 cannot support a refusal under the Regulations.** Regulation 5(6) provides that "any enactment or rule of law that would prevent the disclosure of information in accordance with these Regulations shall not apply". Having correctly found the information to be environmental, NESO cannot then treat section 105 as a bar — and the response's conclusion that the information "does not fall within any of the limited exceptions to that duty" reads as if it were one. Section 105 can only bear on the second limb of regulation 12(5)(e), as a source of confidentiality, and only for information that is in fact confidential: it does not make public information confidential, and it does not relieve NESO of the field-by-field assessment.

**4. The assurance given to IRN respondents does not create a confidentiality the Regulations recognise.** The response relies on customers having been "advised that information they provided in response to the IRN would be treated confidentially". The IRN was issued, in NESO's own words, "under NESO's statutory information gathering powers", with "a complete and accurate response … required from all relevant demand customers". The Commissioner's guidance is clear that a public authority cannot contract out of the Regulations by promising confidentiality, and that an assurance given alongside a compulsory notice carries little weight in a public interest balance, because the information would have been provided regardless. The assurance also promised that the information would be "used solely to inform demand pipeline reform and related policy development, and not used to disadvantage any individual project"; disclosure under a statutory access regime is not NESO using the information, and NESO has identified no project it would disadvantage.

**5. Aggregate, no-names information was available and is NESO's own settled practice.** The response considered aggregation only in respect of the forthcoming IRN Analysis. But NESO has released, to other requesters, precisely the form of information that would answer questions 1 and 4 without naming anyone: the count, size distribution and month of receipt of battery-to-data-centre modifications for England and Wales (FOI/26/121) and Scotland (FOI/25/294, internal review), and transmission-connected demand by firm connection year (FOI/26/113). I ask the reviewer to provide, from the IRN data and the modification list, the same tables by size band, transmission zone, gate status and month, and to treat the harm identified in the response as answered by that form.

**6. The IRN Analysis and regulation 12(4)(d).** I accept that a document in the course of completion may be withheld. Regulation 9 nevertheless requires advice and assistance, and the Commissioner expects an authority relying on 12(4)(d) to say when the document will be published. I ask for the expected publication date, and for the analysis to be sent to me on publication.

**7. Question 3, the DNO data.** The response says the distribution-queue data was "provided to NESO in confidence by the DNOs". A confidence between two authorities about third parties' projects is not a confidentiality "provided by law to protect a legitimate economic interest" of those third parties unless the information is itself confidential; the same field-by-field test applies, and the same no-names table answers it.

**8. The public interest.** The response acknowledges "a public interest in understanding their potential impact on electricity demand and the local areas in which projects are planned", and that is exactly the interest at stake. Ofgem's Curate consultation calibrated a commitment fee and milestones on this data and closed on 16 September 2026, with decisions expected this year; the consultation put data centres at approximately 73 GW across approximately 315 projects and implied £693 billion of capital expenditure, and none of it can be tested from published material. Local planning authorities and the communities they consult decide applications for the same projects without knowing what capacity they have contracted for or under what technology they entered the queue. Against that, the response identifies no project and no particular harm, and the presumption in favour of disclosure under regulation 12(2) is recorded but not applied.

I would be grateful if the review addressed each point. If any exception is maintained, I ask that the response state, for each field withheld, the limb of regulation 12(5)(e) relied on and the harm identified, so that any complaint to the Information Commissioner can be precise.

Yours faithfully,
