**Points 1-4**

We have considered these parts of your request and can confirm that the disclosure of this information is withheld under EIR regulations 12(5)(d) and 12(5)(e).

EIR Regulation 12(5)(d) states that:

> "a public authority may refuse to disclose information to the extent that its disclosure would adversely affect the confidentiality of the proceedings of that or any other public authority where such confidentiality is provided by law;"

The data provided to Ofgem by NESO forms part of the policy-making process to design proposals under its Demand Connections Reform package of reforms. The proceedings by which Ofgem uses data provided by NESO to design proposals is confidential. This confidentiality is due to the common law of confidence with the submission of information reasonably expected to be confidential.

Disclosure of the information relating to the design of proposals would adversely affect the confidentiality of Ofgem's proceedings, as it would reveal information forming part of Ofgem's live assessment and internal deliberative process on the design of appropriate and effective measures to manage the rapid growth of data centres in the demand connections queue. Disclosure would also discourage applicants from providing information to NESO as part of the connections applications process and in turn would adversely affect Ofgem's ability to carry out any assessment of the demand connections queue.

EIR Regulation 12(5)(e) states that:

> "a public authority may refuse to disclose information to the extent that its disclosure would adversely affect the confidentiality of commercial or industrial information where such confidentiality is provided by law to protect a legitimate economic interest."

The information you have requested about the demand queue data provided by NESO concerns commercial information relating to projects in the demand connections queue which are subject to ongoing planning, regulatory and commercial processes.

As set out above, the information has been provided to Ofgem in circumstances giving rise to an expectation of confidence. This confidentiality is provided by law to protect applicants' ongoing legitimate economic interest to continue developing, financing and delivering their projects in a competitive market. Disclosure would breach this confidence and would adversely affect the legitimate economic interests of applicants in the connections queue.

Disclosure would also allow competitors, investors, suppliers, counterparties and other market participants to gain valuable insight into the composition of the queue and the status of other projects. This would place certain projects at a commercial disadvantage compared to competitors and prejudice ongoing and future negotiations and would also result in additional harm through loss of confidence from investors and lenders, damaging the project's commercial standing.

**Points 5 and 6**

We have considered these parts of your request and can confirm that we do not hold this information.

EIR Regulation 12(4)(a) states that:

> "a public authority may refuse to disclose information to the extent that it does not hold that information when an applicant's request is received;"

With respect to the existence of any data-sharing agreement, memorandum of understanding, or confidentiality arrangement between Ofgem and NESO governing Ofgem's receipt and use of demand connection queue data. Ofgem obtains and processes this information by virtue of the provisions of the Utilities Act 2000, and Part 1 of the Electricity Act 1989.

Concerning correspondence on the connections queue data, Ofgem plans to consult further on options proposed in its the December 2025, Connections End-to-end Review: Updated Proposals and Next Steps this Autumn. This will include an option to introduce a requirement for network companies to publish a 'Demand Capacity Register'.

**Public interest test**

In relying on EIR regulation 12(5)(d) and 12 (5)(e), we have had to consider the public interest test. The factors we have considered are set out below.

The factors we have considered are set out below. Please note that disclosure under the EIR is considered to be disclosure to the world at large, not just to you, the requestor.

Factors in favour of disclosure:

- There is a presumption in favour of disclosure under the EIR
- There is a public interest in transparency in relation to Ofgem's role in Demand Connections Reform and composition of the transmission demand connection queue, including the scale and type of projects seeking connection and how this has changed.

Factors in favour of withholding the information:

- There is a strong public interest in preserving the confidentiality of commercially sensitive information where that confidentiality is protected by law
- Disclosure would undermine the legitimate economic interests that the confidentiality is intended to protect by placing commercially sensitive information into the public domain
- Disclosure would adversely affect the commercial position of businesses by revealing information that competitors or other market participants could use to their advantage and to the detriment of companies supplying information to NESO
- Disclosure would adversely affect the confidentiality of the proceedings in how Ofgem conducts ongoing policy development could reduce stakeholders' willingness to share evidence, undermining the future quality of policy-making
- The wider public interest in transparency regarding the Demand Queue can be met through publication of aggregate data rather than disclosure of specific commercially sensitive information about identifiable projects.

Whilst we recognise the public interest in transparency concerning Demand Connections Reform and the growth in data centre demand, we consider that, in this case, greater weight should be given to the public interest in protecting legally confidential commercial information and avoiding harm to the legitimate economic interests of the business concerned. We therefore conclude that the balance of the public interest strongly favours maintaining the exception and withholding this information.
