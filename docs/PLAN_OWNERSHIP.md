# Build plan: ownership above the applicant, in the reader

Written 2026-08-26, the day the Companies House SPV pull landed. It
covers what to build first — a text chain and a three-state tier in the
site page's existing "Who is behind it" section — and, separately,
sketches the richer treatments (flags, logos, a world map) as things to
attempt **only if the data earns them**, with the test each one has to
pass first.

The instinct behind those richer treatments is Luke's and it is a good
one: this is the most genuinely new material the project has produced in
weeks, and a planning record that can say who owns a scheme is doing
something no planning portal does. The argument below is about sequence,
not about whether.

## What we hold

`data/external_sources/companies-house-ownership.yaml` — 111 companies,
generated by `scripts/ch_write_ownership.py`, with charges, PSC,
shareholders (from confirmation statements), corporate officers and
accounts-filed status per company. `companies-house-spvs.yaml` is the
hand-adjudicated half: which company is which scheme, at three
confidence levels (43 confirmed / 42 probable / 22 tentative), 90 mapped
to a site.

Four registers, because no single one answers the question:

| register | answers | silent when |
|---|---|---|
| PSC | who has significant control | the parent is an overseas LP — not a registrable relevant legal entity |
| charges | who lends, which is the most honest statement of control | nothing is secured |
| confirmation statement | who holds the shares | the latest filing is "with no updates" (48 of 111) |
| Register of Overseas Entities | beneficial owner, compelled | the company is UK-registered |

## Decisions already made — do not relitigate

- **No figure from this source becomes a site column.** EXTERNAL_DATA_SOURCES
  §7.1, settled twice. Ownership is claims-and-provenance material like
  every other external source.
- **`has_charges` is never used to decide whether to ask.** It read false
  for 44 of the 49 companies that carry charges. Probe unconditionally.
- **Confidence is carried, not flattened.** A `tentative` company→scheme
  mapping must never render identically to a `confirmed` one.
- **The chain going dark is a finding, not a gap to hide.** "No
  registrable person, because an overseas limited partnership is not a
  registrable relevant legal entity" is publishable; a blank is not.

## Increment 1 — the text chain and the tier

Goes into the site page's **"Who is behind it"** section (handoff README
§3, role/value rows with the counting caveat verbatim) as a fourth row
group **beneath** the existing roles, not replacing them. The Barbour
role blocks answer "who is doing this"; this answers "who owns the thing
doing it", and the reader should be able to see it is a different
question answered by a different source.

**a. The tier, on the row.** Three states, and only three:

- **UK-controlled** — a PSC entity registered in the UK, or a
  controlling individual of UK nationality, and nothing above it
  pointing elsewhere.
- **Overseas-controlled** — a PSC entity registered outside the UK, an
  overseas beneficial owner from the ROE, or a foreign corporate form in
  the shareholder list.
- **Not disclosed** — the register reads "no registrable person", or the
  chain terminates at a partnership that files nothing.

Rationale for a tier rather than a country: the jurisdiction strings are
not normalised and should not be shown to a reporter as though they
were. The same US registry appears as "United States", "Maryland
Corporation Registry", "United States - Maryland Corporation Registry"
and "State Of Maryland Register Of Companies"; one company's
jurisdiction reads "New York Stock Exchange (Nyse)", which is a listing
venue; another reads "Britain"; another "Engalnd And Wales". A tier
survives that. A flag does not. This is the same rule already agreed for
external capacity claims on scannable rows: show the confidence tier,
never the raw value.

**b. The chain, as text, on expand.** One line per link, each carrying
its own source and date:

```
UK Court Lane DC Ltd            company 14045228
  ← UK Court Lane DC Holdings, LP        confirmation statement, 7 of 7 ordinary shares
  ← not disclosed                         PSC register: no registrable person —
                                          an overseas LP is not a registrable
                                          relevant legal entity
  secured against  NFO Holdings, LLC → Trimont Europe Ltd   charges register, May 2026
```

Every link is drillable to the filing it came from. Where two registers
disagree, show both — this project does not average sources.

**c. The caveat, verbatim, as the section already does for roles.**
Draft: *"Ownership is read from Companies House filings for the company
named as applicant or client. Where that company is a single-scheme
vehicle its filings describe this scheme; where it is a group company
they may not. A chain that stops does not mean ownership stops — an
overseas partnership is not required to be registered."*

**d. Scope.** 90 companies map to a site; roughly 43% show a non-UK or
undisclosed controller. Sites with no mapped company show the section
exactly as it renders today, with no empty ownership group — absence of
a filing is not a finding about the site.

**Not in increment 1:** any aggregate count of foreign ownership, on any
page. The 43% is a session-old measurement over an un-normalised field
and is not a publishable figure yet. See the gate below.

## Increment 2 — only if the data earns it

Each of these is worth attempting. None should be attempted before its
gate passes, and the gates are cheap.

**A world map on the start page.** *Gate: the jurisdiction field is
normalised to ISO country codes with a recorded mapping, and the
"not disclosed" population is renderable as a category rather than
omitted.* A map that draws 19 countries and silently drops the 13
companies whose ownership is undisclosed tells a reader the opposite of
the truth — those are the most likely to be foreign-parented, which is
*why* they are undisclosed. If the map cannot draw "we cannot see", it
is not ready. Precedent: the LA-consumption choropleth is parked behind
`PLAN_CONSUMPTION_CONTEXT.md` for the same reason — the per-site
sentence proves the numbers and the caveat language before anything is
painted.

**Flags on the rows.** *Gate: the map ships first, and the tier has
survived a release without a correction.* A flag is a categorical claim
at a glance, on a scannable row, from a field with four spellings of one
registry. It is the highest-confidence rendering available applied to
the lowest-confidence data we hold. If normalisation lands and holds,
this becomes reasonable — as a small mark **beside** the tier, never
instead of it, and never on a "not disclosed" row.

**Company logos.** *Gate: a decision on trademark use in a published
Guardian artefact, and a payload budget that survives it.* Separate from
the data question: logos are trademarks, they would need embedding as
data URIs inside the single-file reader, and they would make a planning
record look like a corporate directory. Recommend deferring
indefinitely; a company name in Source Serif is not the weak version of
a logo.

**An ownership-concentration view.** Not requested, but the more likely
prize than any of the above: several unrelated schemes resolve to the
same ultimate owner (two Starwood fund vintages; eleven Jersey vehicles
to DigitalBridge; Blackstone, Alphabet, CyrusOne, Invesco, Tritax). A
"schemes by ultimate owner" grouping answers a question a reporter
cannot currently ask of any public source. *Gate: the same
normalisation, plus a decision on how to treat a `tentative` mapping in
an aggregate — one wrong link makes a wrong cluster.*

## Order of work

1. Normalise `registered_in` to ISO codes in a recorded mapping file,
   preserving the raw string beside it. This is the unlock for
   everything in increment 2 and is a couple of hours.
2. Increment 1a — the tier, and its three states, in `site_profile`.
3. Increment 1b/c — the chain text and the caveat, in the site page.
4. Measure: how many sites render each tier, and how many chains have at
   least one link. Publish that in the methodology, not as a headline.
5. Re-open increment 2 with the normalised field and a release of
   evidence behind the tier.

## The thing to check before quoting any of this

The 43% figure took three attempts to compute — 71, then 55, then 48 of
111 — because the first two probes matched jurisdiction strings the
register spells inconsistently. Any number derived from this data should
be computed twice by different means before it reaches a reader, and the
counting rule stated beside it.
